One Percent

What would you charge one person, once?

Answer that, and the arithmetic does something uncomfortable: multiply it by fifty thousand people. This is a half-hour call about the number you just produced, and the first customer standing behind it.

$7 a session. Paid before the call. The work is worth a hundred dollars an hour and that is what it is priced at everywhere else. Seven is what is asked here. The difference is deliberate and it is not a discount you have to qualify for.

What happens

  1. A five-minute screening call. Three questions, answered short. What you do that somebody has paid you for, what you would charge one person once, and the one practical thing in the way.
  2. Thirty minutes on your figures. Your rate, the arithmetic run in front of you, who the first paying customer actually is, and what is standing between you and that one person.
  3. One action, with a name against it. Not five good ideas. One thing, and who is doing it. A session that ends in a list ends in nothing.

The eight questions are written down and published in full — read the method before you pay. Nothing is held back for the call and nobody is screened for fitness. The only question is whether seven dollars is worth answering those eight and hearing what I make of your answers on the other end.

The first call is an automated assistant

It says so at the start, and it says the call is recorded by an outside service. Nothing after that first call is recorded by anyone outside this work.

It asks three questions and stops. It will not ask where you are, it will not ask for the background, and if you start telling it what has been done to you it will say that is not what this call is for and end. That is deliberate. A screening call is not the place, and a machine is not the listener.
